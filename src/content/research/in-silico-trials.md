An in-silico clinical trial tests a treatment on a cohort of virtual patients. I led a systematic review of cardiac in-silico trials published between 1 January 2012 and 1 January 2022, which identified 36 studies. A validation step was performed in 75% of them and a sensitivity analysis in 19%, 28% did not report patient demographics, and 97% gave no link to the data or models used. The review calls for community agreement on minimal reporting standards for patient demographics, accepted standards for the quality control of virtual cohorts, uncertainty quantification, and more sharing of models and data ([Rodero et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1088/2516-1091/acdc71)).

I have applied the approach to the design of pacing leads ([Rodero et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.compbiomed.2021.105073)) and contributed to an in-silico trial of sex-specific inclusion criteria for cardiac resynchronisation therapy ([Qian et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41746-026-03139-7)).
